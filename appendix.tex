\subsection{Proof of \Cref{lemma:difference_lemma}}

We present the proof of the difference lemma, formally:

\begin{restatable}[Difference Lemma]{lemma}{difflemma}\label{lemma:difference_lemma}
    Let $\mathcal W_0, \mathcal W_1, \mathcal E$ be events with
    \begin{align}
        \Prob{\mathcal W_0 \land \lnot \mathcal E} = \Prob{\mathcal W_1 \land \lnot \mathcal E} \label{eq:dif}
    \end{align}
    Then we can bound the difference in probability of $\mathcal W_0$ and $\mathcal W_1$ with
    \[
        \abs*{\Prob{\mathcal W_0} - \Prob{\mathcal W_1}} \le \Prob{\mathcal E}
    \]
\end{restatable}

\paragraph{Proof of \Cref{lemma:difference_lemma}.}
Let $\mathcal W_0, \mathcal W_1, \mathcal E$ be events satisfying \eqref{eq:dif}. Then:
\begin{align}
    \abs*{\Prob{\mathcal W_0} - \Prob{\mathcal W_1}}
    =& 
    \big|\Prob{\mathcal W_0 \land \mathcal E} + \Prob{\mathcal W_0 \land \lnot \mathcal E} \\ 
    &\quad - \Prob{\mathcal W_1 \land \mathcal E} - \Prob{\mathcal W_1 \land \lnot \mathcal E}\big|\\ 
    =& \abs*{\Prob{\mathcal W_0 \land \mathcal E} - \Prob{\mathcal W_1 \land \mathcal E}}\\ 
    =& \abs*{\Prob{\mathcal W_0 \mid \mathcal E} \Prob{\mathcal E} - \Prob{\mathcal W_1 \mid \mathcal E} \Prob{\mathcal E}}\\ 
    =& \abs*{\Prob{\mathcal W_0 \mid \mathcal E} - \Prob{\mathcal W_1 \mid \mathcal E}} \Prob{\mathcal E}\\
    \le&\Prob{\mathcal E} 
\end{align}

\subsection{Proof of \Cref{claim:sk_iff_pk}}
\label{sec:sk_iff_pk}

\skiffpk*

\begin{proof}[\Cref{claim:sk_iff_pk}]
    We show $\sk \in \closure_\SK(\tildeSK, \tildePK) \implies \pk \in \closure_\PK(\tildeSK, \tildePK)$:

    By definition of the closure, we have:
    \[
    \sk \in \closure_\SK(\widetilde\SK) \lor \sk \in \bigcup_{\widetilde\pk \in \closure_\PK(\widetilde\PK)} R_\SK(\widetilde\pk)
    \]
    We distinguish two cases: First, assume $\sk \in \closure_\SK(\tildeSK)$, then:
    \[
        \pk \in R_\PK(\sk)
    \]
    Combining this with $\sk \in \closure_\SK(\tildeSK)$, we get:
    \[
        \pk \in \bigcup_{\widetilde\sk \in \tildeSK} R_\PK(\widetilde\sk)
    \]
    And hence $\pk \in \closure_\PK(\tildeSK, \tildePK)$. 
    
    On the other hand, if $\sk \notin \closure_\SK(\tildeSK)$, then:
    \[
        \sk \in \bigcup_{\widetilde\pk \in \closure_\PK(\tildePK)} R_\SK(\widetilde\pk)
    \]
    In particular, we have $\sk \in R_\SK(\widetilde\pk)$ for some $\widetilde\pk$. By definition of $\sim_\PK$, we have
    $\widetilde\pk \sim_\PK \pk$. Therefore $\widetilde\pk \sim_\PK^* \pk$ which means that $\pk \in [\widetilde\pk]_{\sim_\PK^*}$
    for some $\widetilde\pk \in \tildePK$. In the end we obtain $\pk \in \tildePK$.
    The proof for the converse works analogously.
\end{proof}

The last step holds by definition of $H_2^{\lambda, A} = 1$ and $H_3^{\lambda, \A}$.

\subsection{Proof of \Cref{claim:adv_rewriting_3}}

\advrewritingthree*

For this result we rely on the difference lemma which stated that \Cref{lemma:difference_lemma}.
For that we note that the events $H_1^{\lambda, \A}$ and $H_3^{\lambda, \A}$ are equivalent conditioned on 
the event that the secret key $\sk^*$ sampled by the challenger in the pseudorandomness security experiment is 
not in the closure of $\closure_\SK(\tildeSK, \tildePK)$. Since the events are the same conditioned on that event we have in particular that
\[
    \Prob*{H_1^{\lambda, \A} = 1 \mid \sk^* \notin \closure_\SK(\widetilde{\SK}, \widetilde\PK)}
    = 
    \Prob*{H_3^{\lambda, \A} = 1 \mid \sk^* \notin \closure_\SK(\widetilde{\SK}, \widetilde\PK)}
\]
Applying \Cref{lemma:difference_lemma} we obtain the bound:
\begin{align}
    \abs*{\Prob*{H_1^{\lambda, \A} = 1} - \Prob*{H_3^{\lambda, \A} = 1}} &\le \Prob*{\sk^* \in \closure_\SK(\widetilde\SK, \widetilde\PK)}
\end{align}
Applying the definition of $\closure_\SK(\tildeSK, \tildePK)$ and subsequently a union bound we obtain:
\begin{align}
    \abs*{\Prob*{H_1^{\lambda, \A} = 1} - \Prob*{H_3^{\lambda, \A} = 1}} \le \Prob*{\sk^* \in \closure_\SK(\widetilde\SK)} + \Prob*{\bigcup_{\widetilde\pk \in \closure_\PK(\widetilde\PK)} R_\PK(\widetilde\pk)}
\end{align}
We bound the two terms seperately. Specifically we show that in every set on the RHS of the events 
the number of images corresponding to the secret keys is bounded by $|\tildeSK|$ and $|\tildePK|$
respectively. Now assuming that one of those probabilities is non-negligible then the adversary 
could just assume that this case happens and guess the corresponding image with non-negligible probability 
yielding the correct image with probability at least the product of two non-negligible functions which is itself 
a non-negligible function. Since we however know that $\O_{vrf}$ is pseudorandom this cannot be the case, we conclude 
that the probabilities must be negligible each.

\subsection{Conjecture}

We briefly remark on the difficulty of extending the proof of \Cref{thm:impossibility_21_comb}
to the more general case stated in the conjecture. The direct approach fails due to a subtlety:
While we show that the obscure queries are irrelevant per candidate, that does not imply that over multiple candidates 
the obscure queries are jointly irrelevant.
Since joint irrelevance spanning multiple candidate doesn't necessarily need to hold, we 
guarantee that the algorithm $\alg{EvalSim}{VRF}$ returns $\top$ if the candidate key $\sk_{cand}$
is the game's secret key $\sk^*$.
