\section{Deferred Proofs}

\subsection{Proof of \Cref{lemma:difference_lemma}}

We present the proof of the difference lemma, formally:

\begin{restatable}[Difference Lemma]{lemma}{difflemma}\label{lemma:difference_lemma}
    Let $\mathcal W_0, \mathcal W_1, \mathcal E$ be events with
    \[
        \Prob{\mathcal W_0 \land \lnot \mathcal E} = \Prob{\mathcal W_1 \land \lnot \mathcal E} 
    \]
    Then we can bound the difference in probability of $\mathcal W_0$ and $\mathcal W_1$ with
    \[
        \abs*{\Prob{\mathcal W_0} - \Prob{\mathcal W_1}} \le \Prob{\mathcal E}
    \]
\end{restatable}

\paragraph{Proof of \Cref{lemma:difference_lemma}.}
Let $\mathcal W_0, \mathcal W_1, \mathcal E$ be events and assume $\Prob{\mathcal W_0 \land \lnot \mathcal E} = \Prob{\mathcal W_1 \land \lnot \mathcal E}$.
Then we can deduce the following:
\begin{align}
    &\abs*{\Prob{\mathcal W_0} - \Prob{\mathcal W_1}}\\
    =& 
    \abs*{\Prob{\mathcal W_0 \land \mathcal E} + \Prob{\mathcal W_0 \land \lnot \mathcal E} - \Prob{\mathcal W_1 \land \mathcal E} - \Prob{\mathcal W_1 \land \lnot \mathcal E}}\\ 
    =& \abs*{\Prob{\mathcal W_0 \land \mathcal E} - \Prob{\mathcal W_1 \land \mathcal E}}\\ 
    =& \abs*{\Prob{\mathcal W_0 \mid \mathcal E} \Prob{\mathcal E} - \Prob{\mathcal W_1 \mid \mathcal E} \Prob{\mathcal E}}\\ 
    =& \abs*{\Prob{\mathcal W_0 \mid \mathcal E} - \Prob{\mathcal W_1 \mid \mathcal E}} \Prob{\mathcal E}\\
    \le&\Prob{\mathcal E} 
\end{align}


\subsection{Proof of \Cref{claim:sk_iff_pk}}
\label{sec:sk_iff_pk}

We show $\sk \in \closure_\SK(\tildeSK, \tildePK) \implies \pk \in \closure_\PK(\tildeSK, \tildePK)$, the 
converse follows analogously. Assume the left-hand side, i.e. $\sk \in \closure_\SK(\tildeSK,\tildePK)$ and distinguish 
two cases based on the definition of $\closure_\SK(\tildeSK, \tildePK)$.
If $\sk \in \closure_\SK(\tildeSK)$ then since $\pk \in R_\SK(\sk)$ we obtain 
$\pk \in \bigcup_{\widetilde\sk \in \closure_\SK(\tildeSK)} R_\SK(\widetilde\sk)$ and hence $\pk \in \closure_\PK(\tildeSK, \tildePK)$.
On the other hand, if $\sk \in \bigcup_{\widetilde\pk \in \closure_\PK(\tildePK)} R_\PK(\widetilde\pk)$ 
then $\sk \in R_\PK(\widetilde\pk)$ for some $\widetilde\pk \in \closure_\PK(\tildePK)$. By definition of the closure we have 
$\pk \sim_\PK^* \widetilde\pk$, hence $\pk \in \closure_\PK(\tildePK)$ and thus $\pk \in \closure_\PK(\tildeSK, \tildePK)$.\todo{more details}

\subsection{Proof of \Cref{claim:adv_rewriting_1}}

\advrewritingone*

Let $b'$ be the hidden bit in the pseudorandomness security experiment against $\widetilde\O_{uppc}$ then we have that

\begin{align*}
    &2 \cdot \abs*{\Prob*{\Exp{VF}{prf}(\lambda, \A) = 1} - \frac{1}{2}} \\ 
    &= 2 \cdot \abs*{\Prob*{\Exp{VF}{prf}(\lambda, \A) = 1 \mid b' = 0} \Prob*{b' = 0} + \Prob*{\Exp{VF}{prf}(\lambda, \A) = 1 \mid b' = 1} \Prob*{b' = 1} - \frac{1}{2}} \\ 
    &= 2 \cdot \abs*{\frac{1}{2}\Prob*{\Exp{VF}{prf}(\lambda, \A) = 1 \mid b' = 0}  + \frac{1}{2}\Prob*{\Exp{VF}{prf}(\lambda, \A) = 1 \mid b' = 1} - \frac{1}{2}} \\ 
    &= \abs*{\Prob*{\Exp{VF}{prf}(\lambda, \A) = 1 \mid b' = 0} + \Prob*{\Exp{VF}{prf}(\lambda, \A) = 1 \mid b' = 1}  - 1} \\ 
    &= \abs*{\Prob*{\Exp{VF}{prf}(\lambda, \A) = 1 \mid b' = 0} - \Paren*{1 - \Prob*{\Exp{VF}{prf}(\lambda, \A) = 1 \mid b' = 1}}} \\ 
    &= \abs*{\Prob*{\Exp{VF}{prf}(\lambda, \A) = 1 \mid b' = 0} - \Prob*{\Exp{VF}{prf}(\lambda, \A) = 0 \mid b' = 1}} \\ 
    &= \abs*{\Prob*{H_2^{\lambda, \A} = 1} - \Prob*{H_3^{\lambda, \A} = 1}} 
\end{align*}

\subsection{Proof of \Cref{claim:adv_rewriting_3}}

\advrewritingthree*

For this result we rely on the difference lemma which stated that \Cref{lemma:difference_lemma}.
For that we note that the events $H_1^{\lambda, \A}$ and $H_3^{\lambda, \A}$ are equivalent conditioned on 
the event that the secret key $\sk^*$ sampled by the challenger in the pseudorandomness security experiment is 
not in the closure of $\closure_\SK(\tildeSK, \tildePK)$. Since the events are the same conditioned on that event we have in particular that
\[
    \Prob*{H_1^{\lambda, \A} = 1 \mid \sk^* \notin \closure_\SK(\widetilde{\SK}, \widetilde\PK)}
    = 
    \Prob*{H_3^{\lambda, \A} = 1 \mid \sk^* \notin \closure_\SK(\widetilde{\SK}, \widetilde\PK)}
\]
Applying \Cref{lemma:difference_lemma} we obtain the bound:
\begin{align}
    \abs*{\Prob*{H_1^{\lambda, \A} = 1} - \Prob*{H_3^{\lambda, \A} = 1}} &\le \Prob*{\sk^* \in \closure_\SK(\widetilde\SK, \widetilde\PK)}
\end{align}
Applying the definition of $\closure_\SK(\tildeSK, \tildePK)$ and subsequently a union bound we obtain:
\begin{align}
    \abs*{\Prob*{H_1^{\lambda, \A} = 1} - \Prob*{H_3^{\lambda, \A} = 1}} \le \Prob*{\sk^* \in \closure_\SK(\widetilde\SK)} + \Prob*{\bigcup_{\widetilde\pk \in \closure_\PK(\widetilde\PK)} R_\PK(\widetilde\pk)}
\end{align}
We bound the two terms seperately. Specifically we show that in every set on the RHS of the events 
the number of images corresponding to the secret keys is bounded by $|\tildeSK|$ and $|\tildePK|$
respectively. Now assuming that one of those probabilities is non-negligible then the adversary 
could just assume that this case happens and guess the corresponding image with non-negligible probability 
yielding the correct image with probability at least the product of two non-negligible functions which is itself 
a non-negligible function. Since we however know that $\O_{vrf}$ is pseudorandom this cannot be the case, we conclude 
that the probabilities must be negligible each.

\subsection{Conjecture}

We quickly want to make a few remarks towards the idea of extending the proof of \Cref{lemma:warmup}
to the more general case of \Cref{conjecture}. The direct proof fails because of the subtle reason that 
the hidden oracle queries need to be candidate-wide irrelevant but not jointly irrelevant over queries to 
many distinct candidate schemes. In the proof of \Cref{lemma:warmup} we argue that for oracle $\O_2$
all hidden oracle queries, i.e. all oracle queries not contained in the transcript of the combiners
verification algorithm are exactly the queries that would influence the combiner output if changes.
On the other hand, for any arbitrary assignment of the hidden queries, the output of the combiner 
stays constant as long as the non-hidden queries and the queries to $\O_1$ are honestly answered.

This idea gives rise to the attack of iteratively filtering the secret key space on a polynomial number 
of points by keeping in every round $(1.)$ at least the true secret key, $(2.)$ only secret keys 
for which the output of the combiner is the same as for the true secret key. We argue that after a sufficient 
number of filtering steps, any remaining candidate has sufficiently high chance of evaluating to the same output 
as the combiner does with the true secret key on a uniformly random evaluation point.

However, since the hidden queries must not necessarily be jointly-irrelevant this argument cannot be applied if 
we have more candidates, of which still $n-1$ need to be secure for the combiner to be required to 
implement unique provability and pseudorandomness.
