The issue why this breaks is the following case:
Assume for two oracles $\O_j, \O_k$ there exist obscure queries 
and both are secure vrf oracles. Then we cannot expect that by 
branching over both sets of obscure oracle queries that the output 
for the true secret key $\sk^*$ is $y$ over all branches.

That is because the obscure queries of a secure vrf oracle $\O_i$
are only oracle-wide jointly-irrelevant. If we would follow the proof 
over two changed oracles $\O_j, \O_k$ then we'd arrive at a point 
where we would need to consider the second execution which is possibly 
not uniquely provable or perfectly correct and therefore violate 
the robustness condition, breaking the contradiction.

We \emph{still} know that oracle-wide joint-independence holds however.
Assume $f_t$ is the PPT Turing machine in the terminal node and fix the 
inputs $\mathbf y$ corresponding to oracle outputs to \emph{exposed}
queries of $\O_{vrf}$ or queries to $\O_{uppc}$. Then if we 
group the remaining \emph{obscure} queries to $\O_{vrf}$ by oracle:

\begin{align*}
	f_t(\underbrace{\mathbf C}_{\text{known queries}}, \underbrace{\mathbf y_1^{(1)}, \ldots, \mathbf y_{k_1}^{(1)}}_{\text{obscure queries to }\O_1}, \ldots, \underbrace{\mathbf y_1^{(n)}, \ldots, \mathbf y_{k_n}^{(n)}}_{\text{obscure queries to }\O_n}) = f_t(\mathbf C, \vec{\mathbf y}^{(1)}, \ldots, \vec{\mathbf y}^{(n)})
\end{align*}

We know that, by oracle-wide joint irrelevance, $f_t$ must satisfy:

\begin{align*}
	\forall k \in [n], \forall j \in [n], \forall \vec{\mathbf y}^{(j)}, \vec{\mathbf z}^{(k)} \text{ well formed}, f_t(\mathbf C, \vec{\mathbf y}^{(1)}, \ldots, \vec{\mathbf y}^{(k)}, \ldots, \vec{\mathbf y}^{(n)})
	= f_t(\mathbf C, \vec{\mathbf y}^{(1)}, \ldots, \vec{\mathbf z}^{(k)}, \ldots, \vec{\mathbf y}^{(n)})
\end{align*}

We say the function corresponding to $f_t$ is \emph{axis-constant} since for every single possible input (with fixed $\mathbf C$) the output remains constant if we keep at least $n-1$ inputs constant. If there doesn't exist a robust $(2;3)$ black-box VRF-combiner than this means that there exist two input values such that keeping the third constant, there exist two different, well-formed assignments which results in a different output. That is:

\begin{align*}
	\exists k_1, k_2 \in [3], \forall j \in [3], \forall \vec{\mathbf y}^{(j)}, \vec{\mathbf z}^{(k_1)}, \vec{\mathbf z}^{(k_2)} \text{ well formed}, f_t(\mathbf C, \vec{\mathbf y}^{(j)}, \vec{\mathbf y}^{(k_1)}, \vec{\mathbf y}^{(k_2)}) \neq f_t(\mathbf C, \vec{\mathbf y}^{(j)}, \vec{\mathbf z}^{(k_1)}, \vec{\mathbf z}^{(k_2)})
\end{align*}

\paragraph{The idea.}
The obstruction to extending the proof to the $(n-1)$-out-of-$n$
setting is that arbitrary combinations of obscure-query answers
need not correspond to any globally valid execution of the VRF
oracles. In particular, after independently branching over obscure
queries to two secure oracles $\O_j,\O_k$, the resulting mixed
assignment may violate either perfect correctness or unique
provability and therefore fail to correspond to any realizable
secure VRF-family.

To avoid this issue, we restrict branching to assignments that are
\emph{globally realizable}. Intuitively, every branch considered by
the simulator must admit a completion into a family of secure VRF
oracles satisfying perfect correctness and unique provability.

Formally, let $\mathcal T$ denote the partial transcript generated
during the execution. The simulator maintains the following sets:

\begin{align*}
    \mathcal K &:= \{ (\sk,\pk) \}, \\
    \mathcal E &:= \{ ((\sk,x),(y,\pi)) \}, \\
    \mathcal V &:= \{ ((\pk,x,y,\pi),b) \}.
\end{align*}

Here:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $(\sk,\pk) \in \mathcal K$ records generated key pairs,
    \item $((\sk,x),(y,\pi)) \in \mathcal E$ records evaluation
    queries,
    \item $((\pk,x,y,\pi),b) \in \mathcal V$ records verification
    queries.
\end{itemize}

We say that a partial transcript $\mathcal T$ is
\emph{globally consistent} if it admits an extension to a family of
secure VRF oracles satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item perfect correctness,
    \item unique provability,
    \item consistency of repeated oracle queries.
\end{enumerate}

Equivalently, $\mathcal T$ must satisfy the closure conditions
induced by VRF correctness:

\begin{itemize}
    \item If $((\pk,x,y,\pi),1) \in \mathcal V$, then for every
    $(\sk,\pk) \in \mathcal K$ we must have
    $((\sk,x),(y,\pi)) \in \mathcal E$.

    \item If $((\sk,x),(y,\pi)) \in \mathcal E$, then for every
    $(\sk,\pk) \in \mathcal K$ we must have
    $((\pk,x,y,\pi),1) \in \mathcal V$.
\end{itemize}

Branching is then restricted to assignments preserving global
consistency.

Under this restriction, every obscure-query assignment considered by
the proof corresponds to a realizable secure VRF-family. Hence the
space of admissible assignments becomes closed under single-coordinate
replacement. Consequently, oracle-wise joint irrelevance implies full
joint irrelevance by a standard telescoping argument over the
coordinates.

Assume there exist two admissible globally consistent assignments
$\mathbf y,\mathbf z$ such that
\[
f_t(\mathbf C,\mathbf y) \neq f_t(\mathbf C,\mathbf z).
\]
Since the admissible assignment space is closed under
single-coordinate replacement, one may interpolate between
$\mathbf y$ and $\mathbf z$ by changing one oracle-coordinate at a
time. Oracle-wise joint irrelevance implies that the value of $f_t$
remains invariant at every step, yielding a contradiction.
